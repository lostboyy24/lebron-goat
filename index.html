Part One

The Story

Every great career has an origin. LeBron’s starts with a 16-year-old mother, a series of Akron apartments, and a youth football coach who handed a nine-year-old a basketball.

Early life · 1984–2003

Akron and St. Vincent–St. Mary: “The Chosen One”

LeBron Raymone James was born December 30, 1984, in Akron, Ohio, to 16-year-old Gloria James. The family moved often and struggled; when LeBron was a boy, he lived for a time with the family of youth football coach Frank Walker, who introduced him to basketball around age nine.

Playing AAU ball for the Northeast Ohio Shooting Stars, LeBron and three friends — Dru Joyce III, Sian Cotton and Willie McGee, the “Fab Four” — promised to stay together and enrolled at St. Vincent–St. Mary, a private Catholic school in Akron. The decision drew local controversy. The results did not: the Fighting Irish went 27–0 and won the Division III state title when LeBron was a 6-foot-2 freshman, then won it again as a sophomore (26–1).

By his junior year he was a national name. In February 2002 Sports Illustrated put him on its cover under the headline “The Chosen One,” making him the first high school underclassman on the cover, and a year later his games were being shown on ESPN2 and even sold on pay-per-view in Ohio. Few players have ever been asked to live up to that much hype before turning 18.

He finished high school with three Ohio Mr. Basketball awards, two Gatorade National Player of the Year awards, and the 2003 Naismith Prep Player of the Year, and a McDonald’s All-American Game MVP. His final-year averages: 31.6 points, 9.6 rebounds, 4.6 assists and 3.4 steals per game.

  • Sophomore: 25.2 ppg · first sophomore Ohio Mr. Basketball and USA Today All-USA First Team
  • Junior: 29.0 ppg · SI cover · first junior Gatorade National Player of the Year

Cleveland Cavaliers · 2003–2010

No. 1 Pick. Rookie of the Year. A franchise reborn.

The Cleveland Cavaliers made James the first overall pick of the 2003 NBA Draft, straight out of high school. He chose No. 23 in tribute to Michael Jordan, and in his first game scored 25 points in Sacramento — the most ever by a prep-to-pro player in his debut. He averaged 20.9 points, 5.5 rebounds and 5.9 assists and became the first Cavalier ever to win Rookie of the Year, and only the third player to average 20/5/5 as a rookie.

The next year he was an All-Star at 20, and on March 20, 2005 he dropped 56 on Toronto, then a Cleveland record. In 2006 he led the Cavs to their first playoff berth since 1998, and recorded a triple-double in his playoff debut.

Then came the night that changed how the league saw him. In Game 5 of the 2007 Eastern Conference Finals against the Detroit Pistons, James scored 48 points in a 109–107 double-overtime win, including Cleveland’s last 25 points and the game-winning layup with 2.2 seconds left. It remains one of the greatest playoff performances ever, and it sent Cleveland to its first NBA Finals — a four-game sweep by the Spurs at the hands of a defense designed to dare him to shoot.

He learned. He won the 2007–08 scoring title (30.0 ppg), became Cleveland’s all-time leading scorer, and then put together back-to-back MVP seasons in 2009 and 2010. The 2008–09 Cavaliers won a franchise-record 66 games; James averaged 28.4 points, 7.6 rebounds and 7.2 assists, made his first All-Defensive team and finished second in Defensive Player of the Year voting. In the 2009 playoffs he averaged 35.3 points.

Miami Heat · 2010–2014

The Decision, the villain years, and two championships

On July 8, 2010, James announced on a live ESPN special, The Decision, that he was joining Dwyane Wade and Chris Bosh in Miami. The special raised millions for charity but was widely criticized, Cleveland owner Dan Gilbert published an angry open letter, and for a year James was one of the most disliked athletes in America. He has since said he regrets how it was handled.

The first season ended in a Finals loss to Dallas, a series in which his scoring dropped sharply. What followed is the most important arc of his career: he rebuilt his game with Hakeem Olajuwon, developed a post game and a reliable jumper, and returned as a different player.

2011–12: his third MVP. In the playoffs, with Bosh injured, the Heat went small and James scored 45 points with 15 rebounds in a must-win Game 6 against Boston. Miami beat Oklahoma City in five games for his first championship; James was Finals MVP (28.6 points, 10.2 rebounds, 7.4 assists).

2012–13: a league-best 66–16 record, a 27-game winning streak, shooting 56.5% from the field, and a fourth MVP — one vote shy of being unanimous. In an epic seven-game Finals against San Antonio he recorded two triple-doubles, including Game 6, and the Heat won Game 7 for a second straight title and second straight Finals MVP.

In 2013–14 he scored a career-high 61 points against Charlotte and led Miami to a fourth consecutive Finals, where a dominant Spurs team ended the three-peat bid in five games.

Cleveland Cavaliers · 2014–2018

The Return and the Promise Kept

In June 2014 James opted out in Miami and returned home, switching back to No. 23. The reaction could not have been more different from 2010. Cleveland had gone 52 years without a major pro sports championship, and he had promised to change that.

The Cavaliers reached the 2015 Finals despite injuries to Kevin Love and Kyrie Irving, and a short-handed Cleveland took a 2–1 lead on the Golden State Warriors before falling in six. Then came 2016.

The Warriors had just won a record 73 games. They led the Finals 3–1. No team in NBA Finals history had ever come back from that deficit. James scored 41 points in Game 5 and 41 in Game 6, and in Game 7 he posted 27 points, 11 rebounds and 11 assists. With under two minutes left and the game tied, he chased down Andre Iguodala and swatted the layup off the backboard — The Block. Kyrie Irving hit the shot that followed. Cleveland won 93–89.

James averaged 29.7 points, 11.3 rebounds, 8.9 assists, 2.6 steals and 2.3 blocks in the series, led both teams in all five of those categories, and was the unanimous Finals MVP. It was Cleveland’s first title in 52 years.

The run did not end there. In 2017 he passed Michael Jordan as the NBA’s all-time playoff scoring leader and became the first player to average a triple-double in a Finals (33.6 points, 12.0 rebounds, 10.0 assists) — in a losing effort against the Kevin Durant-era Warriors. In 2018 he carried a rebuilt roster to a fourth straight Finals, scoring 51 in Game 1, for his eighth consecutive Finals.

Los Angeles Lakers · 2018–2026

Year 16 to Year 23: a third franchise, a fourth ring, and the scoring crown

James signed with the Lakers in 2018 and, after an injury-hit first year, teamed with Anthony Davis in 2019–20. He moved to full-time point guard and led the league in assists (10.2) for the first time. When Kobe Bryant died in January 2020, James passed him on the all-time scoring list a day earlier and later delivered an emotional tribute.

In the Orlando “bubble,” the Lakers beat the Heat in six games for the 2020 championship. James averaged 29.8 points, 11.8 rebounds and 8.5 assists in the Finals and became the only player to win Finals MVP with three different franchises,. It was his fourth ring and fourth Finals MVP.

Age did not stop the records. In 2021–22, at 37, he averaged 30.3 points, recorded his 100th triple-double, and became the first player ever to reach 10,000 career points, 10,000 rebounds and 10,000 assists.

On February 7, 2023, in a game against Oklahoma City, he scored 38 points and passed Kareem Abdul-Jabbar’s 38,387 to become the NBA’s all-time leading scorer. The game stopped for a ceremony and Kareem handed him the ball.

He won the inaugural NBA Cup (In-Season Tournament) in December 2023 and was named tournament MVP. On March 2, 2024 he became the first player in NBA history to reach 40,000 points. That summer, at 39, he captained Team USA to a third Olympic gold medal in Paris, carried the American flag at the opening ceremony, and was named Olympic MVP.

On October 22, 2024 he and his son Bronny became the first father-son duo to play in an NBA game together. In 2025–26 he became the first player to appear in 23 NBA seasons, passed Kareem for most career field goals made and most career wins, passed Robert Parish for the most games played in league history, and reached 12,000 career assists — all while being named an All-Star for the 22nd time.

Team USA

Three Olympic golds across 16 years

James debuted for the U.S. at 19 in 2004 (bronze), won another bronze at the 2006 FIBA World Championship, and then helped redeem American basketball: gold in Beijing 2008, gold in London 2012 (where he became the all-time leading scorer in U.S. men’s Olympic history), and gold in Paris 2024 as captain (98–87 over France in the final). In Paris he led the team in rebounds and assists, recorded his second Olympic triple-double — a first in Olympic history — and was named FIBA Olympic MVP. In 2012 he joined Michael Jordan as the only players to win an NBA MVP, NBA title, Finals MVP and Olympic gold in the same year.

Part Three

The Case for LeBron

Greatest of all time is a question about total value — how high, how long, how complete and how often it counted. On each of those, LeBron stands alone or nearly alone.

1

Longevity at an elite level

Twenty-three seasons played, a 24th ahead. He has made the All-Star team 22 times and an All-NBA team 21 times — every year from 2005 through 2025. At 37 he averaged 30.3 points. At 38 he became the scoring king. At 39 he was an Olympic MVP. At 41 he was still an All-Star.

  • Points per game for 23 seasons: never below 20.9
  • Scoring crown not from one peak but from sustained excellence
  • Only player over 50,000 combined points
2

Versatility no one can match

He is the NBA’s all-time leading scorer and fourth in career assists, sixth in steals and the only member of the 10,000-points / rebounds / assists club. He has played every position, been an All-Defensive First Team selection five times, and was the player Miami trusted to guard all five positions.

  • Scorer: scoring title and the all-time points record
  • Playmaker: assists champion, a 10-assist point guard at 35
  • Defender: 5 All-Defensive First Teams, 2nd in DPOY voting (2009)
3

A towering peak

Not just a longevity player. He was All-NBA First Team in 12 of the 13 seasons from 2006 to 2018. John Hollinger called his 2008–09 season “arguably the greatest individual season in history,” and ESPN ranked his 2012 postseason second-best in modern NBA playoff history.

  • 4 MVPs in 5 years (2009–2013), like Bill Russell the only player to do so
  • 2012–13: 66 wins, 27-game streak, 56.5% FG
  • 2016 playoffs: statistical leader of both teams in all five main categories in the Finals
4

An unprecedented Finals résumé

Ten Finals between 2007 and 2020, eight straight, and a career Finals line of 28.4 points, 10.2 rebounds and 7.8 assists in 55 games. He won Finals MVP for three different franchises and holds the record for Finals triple-doubles. His 2016 title came on the only 3–1 comeback in Finals history.

  • 4 titles with 3 franchises, each as the best player on the floor
  • Finals triple-double average (2017) in a series he lost
  • Of his six Finals losses, three came against the Warriors and two against the Spurs

Part Four

The Counterarguments

A fair case has to meet the other side. Here is what Michael Jordan’s supporters say — and how LeBron’s supporters answer.

Jordan — “6–0 in the Finals”

Jordan won six titles in six Finals appearances, took Finals MVP every time, and never lost on the biggest stage. James is 4–6.

Rebuttal

It is a strong point for Jordan and an honest one. But James’s six losses came to San Antonio (2007, 2014), Dallas (2011) and Golden State (2015, 2017, 2018), and he averaged 28.4/10.2/7.8 across all 55 Finals games. And he reached the Finals ten times to Jordan’s six, including eight in a row; getting there that often, in a conference and era that kept producing rivals, is itself an achievement.

Jordan — “Peak scoring and defense”

Jordan won 10 scoring titles, a Defensive Player of the Year award, and holds the highest career scoring average in NBA history (30.1) and the highest playoff average (33.4).

Rebuttal

Jordan was the more explosive pure scorer. James counters with volume and balance: more rebounds and assists, far more total production, and a playoff scoring average of 28.2 held over 302 games versus Jordan’s 179. Jordan’s peak is the highest scoring peak; James’s combined peak-plus-longevity is the largest body of work.

“LeBron joined superteams”

The Decision, the Big Three in Miami, and later alliances with Kyrie Irving, Kevin Love and Anthony Davis are cited as shortcuts.

Rebuttal

Jordan also played with Hall of Famers like Scottie Pippen, and James took a cast of role players to the 2007 Finals and a 2018 Finals with a rebuilt roster. His 2016 title required the largest comeback in Finals history against a 73-win team. Choosing where to play is part of how every star builds a winner — and his moves changed player empowerment in the league.

“Kareem and Russell won more, or longer”

Kareem Abdul-Jabbar won six MVPs and six titles; Bill Russell won 11 championships.

Rebuttal

Russell played in a much smaller, very different league; Kareem’s scoring record was the one LeBron passed. James has the all-time points record, a more complete box-score profile than either, and has played in the modern, global, more athletic NBA. Era comparisons cut both ways, but he leads in nearly every counting category.

“His longevity is just compiling”

Critics say the scoring record is a product of playing 23 seasons, not of ever being the best.

Rebuttal

Longevity is a skill. He stayed at an All-NBA level for 21 straight seasons, and his 26.8-point career average already includes his twilight seasons. The record is not just volume: he was also the best player in the world, per ESPN and Sports Illustrated, through most of the 2010s.

The honest verdict

Michael Jordan’s perfect Finals record, ten scoring titles and Defensive Player of the Year trophy give him a legitimate claim to the title. LeBron James has the larger body of work: more points, more assists, more playoff production, more Finals appearances, more seasons at an elite level, and a career not yet over. If greatness is peak plus everything else, James is the answer. In national polls Jordan still leads, but the gap narrows sharply with younger fans.

Part Seven

Beyond the Game

The same hometown that shaped him is the one he has worked to change.

I PROMISE School

Opened July 30, 2018 in Akron as a public elementary school created in a partnership between the LeBron James Family Foundation and Akron Public Schools to help struggling students stay in school. James has called it his most important professional accomplishment.

The Family Foundation

Based in Akron, the foundation runs an annual bike-a-thon (since 2005), and built the I Promise Village housing complex, the House Three-Thirty community center, and the I Promise Health Quarters medical center. A 2015 partnership with the University of Akron promised scholarships for up to 2,300 students.

Recognition

The NBA gave James its J. Walter Kennedy Citizenship Award in 2017 for service to the community. He has long been outspoken on social issues and in 2020 backed the voting-rights group More Than a Vote.

Business

His seven-year, roughly $90 million Nike deal came before he played an NBA game; a minority stake in Liverpool F.C. grew into a partnership in Fenway Sports Group; early investments in Beats and Blaze Pizza paid off. In 2021 he became the first active NBA player to reach $1 billion in career earnings.

Media

With business partner Maverick Carter he co-founded SpringHill (More Than a Game, Space Jam: A New Legacy, The Redeem Team), Uninterrupted and the talk show The Shop.

Family

James married Savannah Brinson in 2013. They have three children: Bronny, Bryce and Zhuri. In 2024 Bronny was drafted by the Lakers, and father and son played together.
